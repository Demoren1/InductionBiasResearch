\subsection{Wider experimental selection}
\label{app:asrr-tuning-details}

The wider comparison retains the source-only families from
Appendix~\ref{app:asrr-experiments} at 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512,
1024, 2048, 4096 and 8192 codes, where available. Family and budget
selection use development episodes directly; source M does not first
remove all but one candidate at a budget. The earlier successful
indexators remain fixed reference candidates. Practical G34 uses
retained banks and their source-only capacity refinements; this is not
a new exhaustive structural search.

An initial screen uses 48 development episodes and 200 Adam updates.
With within-task numerical validation, three learning rates
(0.003, 0.01 and 0.03) and two starts give six numerical fits. For
support-only tasks, development also compares activity quantiles
0.1, 0.5 and 0.9. It fixes a common numerical rule rather than selecting
states on a target query. The best three candidates per family advance,
ranked by development accuracy, then cross-entropy and fewer codes.
The original successful aSRR candidate and Dense always remain eligible.

The best screened seed in each of the five original aSRR families and
in practical G34 also receives positive-scale source-weight projection.
We use 12 updates through 1024 codes and four at larger budgets, retaining
every unmodified seed. These refinements enter the final development
comparison on 96 episodes.

With numerical validation, this stage compares the original six-start
200-update rule, a 24-start version, and a wider 24-start grid using
rates 0.001/0.003/0.01/0.03 and quantiles 0.1/0.5/0.9 at horizons
200, 500 and 1000. Numerical-validation loss chooses checkpoints and
starts within each task. The query prediction uses either the best
retained start or the average class probabilities of the best four;
development chooses this rule. The original Omniglot six-start rule
retains its original bias initialization.

Without numerical validation, a 1000-update development trace supplies
three learning-rate, quantile and fixed-update rules. Each trains a
nested pool of 16 starts, and development compares probability averages
over the first one, four or 16 members. Target fitting uses the frozen
rule on support labels only. All 16 starts are charged to training even
when fewer members form the inference ensemble.

The primary policy maximizes development accuracy, with cross-entropy
and fewer codes breaking ties. A second policy minimizes development
cross-entropy and is reported separately. All choices are frozen before
two fresh cohorts, with no intervening retuning. Both use the same
class or subject splits as the preceding comparisons. The 100+50 image
settings use 100 fitting and 50 numerical-selection examples per class;
Omniglot uses five-way tasks with 10 fitting, five selection and five
query images per class. The smaller image and HAR settings receive no
additional numerical-selection labels. Numerical values are always
fresh; only the fixed indexator transfers.

Omniglot uses the retained disjoint character split with 1028 source,
172 development and 423 target characters. Images are inverted,
resized to $28\times28$ and divided by 255. Each episode chooses five
characters and one multiple-of-90-degree rotation per character, then
splits its 20 drawings into fitting, numerical-selection and query sets.
The network is 784--16--5, with no supplied spatial structure.
Its bank reuses the 16 Dense source tasks with lowest numerical-selection
cross-entropy among 128 earlier five-way source episodes. Each source
episode has five fitting, five numerical-selection and ten
structure-selection drawings per class. Four retained numerical fits
supply aligned profiles and their uncertainty. Source preparation uses
the earlier augmented episodes; the new development and target draws
use only the character rotation described above.
