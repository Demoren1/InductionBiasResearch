\subsection{Broader selection of aSRR banks}
\label{sec:asrr-broader}

The preceding comparison limits compressed banks to 512 codes. We also
evaluate a wider search over 16--8192 codes and retain all aSRR families
for direct development selection. Each retained aSRR, practical G34 and
Dense candidate receives the same applicable numerical search; the
methods have different numbers of structural candidates. G34 includes
source-only refinements. This comparison evaluates these three procedures
separately from the other structural methods in the preceding tables.

First, the previously fixed successful procedures were repeated on
512 fresh episodes per dataset. With 100 fitting and 50 additional
numerical-selection images per class, MNIST aSRR with 1024 codes reaches 96.27\%,
compared with 96.02\% for Dense24 and 96.09\% for G34. In five-way
Omniglot~\cite{lake2015human} with 10 fitting and five numerical-selection
images per class,
the corresponding accuracies are 81.75\%, 81.16\% and 79.95\%,
using 2048 aSRR codes.
These replications use the original indexators and numerical procedures.

The wider search then screens candidates on 48 development episodes
and trains finalists on 96 development episodes. It compares numerical
learning rules with up to 1000 updates and 24 starts. Without separate
within-task validation, development fixes a single numerical rule and
the size of a probability ensemble; no extra target labels are used.
Otherwise, within-task validation selects numerical checkpoints and
starts. Development accuracy selects the primary procedure, with
cross-entropy and fewer codes breaking ties. A separate cross-entropy
choice is retained for analysis. Appendix~\ref{app:asrr-tuning-details}
specifies the search and its numerical costs.

All choices are frozen before two fresh cohorts, with no retuning between
them. Each cohort contains 512 episodes per image setting and 90 HAR
episodes. Tables~\ref{tab:asrr-tuned-small} and
\ref{tab:asrr-tuned-validated} pool the unchanged procedures across
these cohorts. The class and subject environments were studied before;
intervals remain conditional on them.

The largest paired gains over Dense are on Omniglot,
$2.25\pm0.34$ percentage points, and HAR, $5.31\pm1.69$ percentage
points. Both gains are positive in each cohort separately. On Omniglot,
aSRR also improves over G34 by $1.73\pm0.35$ percentage points; on HAR,
the difference from G34 is not established by the subject-level interval.
All intervals here are paired 95\% intervals.

On MNIST with 16 fitting images, aSRR improves over both controls;
with 100+50 images it improves over Dense without establishing a
difference from G34. On Fashion 100+50 the difference from Dense is
not established, while the primary Fashion 16 policy loses
$0.11\pm0.05$ percentage points. The separately frozen cross-entropy
policies are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:asrr-tuning-details}.

These gains retain a smaller numerical representation after accounting
for ensembles. Omniglot uses 4500 aSRR parameters versus 50\,580 for
Dense; HAR uses 3936 versus 145\,504. The fixed indexator is excluded
from these counts. Thus wider candidate coverage and numerical selection
can change the conclusion of a restricted-budget comparison, while the
benefit of sharing remains dependent on the task setting.
