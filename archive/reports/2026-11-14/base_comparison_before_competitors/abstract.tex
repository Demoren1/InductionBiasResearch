The connectivity and parameter-sharing structure of a neural network
influences generalization from limited data. We study how fitted
solutions to related tasks can reveal constraints for new tasks whose
numerical parameters are learned afresh. An indexator specifies
permitted connections and groups of shared weights, while a trainable
codebook supplies task-specific values. We characterize when aligned
ReLU weights identify these groups and how partial information from
several tasks can resolve individual ambiguities. We compare joint
calibrated projection with direct recovery from weight ratios under
shared source preparation and numerical selection protocols. Both
recover the generating indexator in two controlled synthetic cases.
On class-disjoint MNIST and Fashion-MNIST tasks, the selected constraints
use fewer numerical parameters with lower accuracy than Dense, and
direct recovery trails joint projection. These results distinguish
conditional identifiability from the practical usefulness of sharing.
