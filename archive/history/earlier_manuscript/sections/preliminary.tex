\section{Preliminary}
\label{sec:preliminary}

\subsection{Multi-task learning}
\label{sec:tasks}

Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a family of tasks with common input and target spaces
$\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$. Each task $\tau\in\mathcal{T}$ is associated
with a distribution $P_\tau$ on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$, while
a distribution $p(\tau)$ over $\mathcal{T}$ describes the task environment
\cite{baxter2000model}.
Learning across this environment allows information from observed tasks
to inform learning on new ones.

We distinguish disjoint, nonempty finite collections of training and test tasks,
$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{te}}$.
For each task, a support sample $S_\tau^{\mathrm{s}}$ is used to learn
its predictor, and an independent query sample $S_\tau^{\mathrm{q}}$
is used to assess its performance; both consist of independent
observations from $P_\tau$ \cite{finn2017model,zhou2021meta}.
For a predictor $f$, a nonempty sample $S$, and a prediction loss $\ell$,
the empirical loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_S(f)
    :=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{(x,y)\in S}\ell(f(x),y).
    \label{eq:task-loss}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Masked predictors and network representations}
\label{sec:masked-predictors}

Let $f_{\theta,M}$ be a neural network with numerical parameters $\theta$
and a binary mask $M\in\{0,1\}^{d\times h}$ applied to the incoming weight
matrix $W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times h}$ of a hidden layer with $d$ inputs
and $h$ neurons. The effective weights are
$\widetilde W=W\odot M$, where $\odot$ denotes elementwise multiplication.
For an integer connection budget $1\le K\le dh$, the admissible masks are
$\mathcal{M}_K:=\{M\in\{0,1\}^{d\times h}:\|M\|_0=K\}$.

The mask specifies the available connections, while their values are
learned separately for each task. We write $\widehat\theta_\tau(M)$ for
the parameters obtained by fitting a task-specific predictor on
$S_\tau^{\mathrm{s}}$ under a common learning rule, with $M$ held fixed.
The effective weights and their loss gradients provide weight-space
features of the resulting predictor. For a sample $S$, define
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_S(\theta,M):=\nabla_W\mathcal{L}_S(f_{\theta,M}),
    \label{eq:weight-gradient}
\end{equation}
where differentiation is with respect to $W$ at a fixed mask $M$.

Such features depend on the numbering of hidden neurons, although
this ordering has no effect on the network function.
Let $b\in\mathbb{R}^h$ be the hidden biases and
$W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times r}$ the weights connecting the hidden layer
to the next layer of width $r$.
For a layer input $u\in\mathbb{R}^d$, an elementwise activation $\sigma$,
and any permutation matrix $\Pi\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times h}$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        &(\Pi^{\mathsf T}W_2)^{\mathsf T}
        \sigma\!\left(
            (\widetilde W\Pi)^{\mathsf T}u+\Pi^{\mathsf T}b
        \right) \\
        &\qquad= W_2^{\mathsf T}\sigma(\widetilde W^{\mathsf T}u+b).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:hidden-permutation}
\end{equation}
Thus, a consistent permutation of incoming weights, biases, and outgoing
weights leaves the predictor unchanged. The same column permutation
applies to $W$, $M$, and the loss gradients.

Neural Functional (NF) layers account for this symmetry by producing
weight-space features that transform equivariantly under hidden-neuron
permutations \cite{zhou2023permutation}.
Averaging these features over hidden neurons yields a representation
independent of their ordering, making NF layers suitable for encoding
solutions learned by different predictors.

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

Our aim is to learn a shared connectivity pattern that serves as a
structural inductive bias for new tasks. The available training data are
$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}:=\{(S_\tau^{\mathrm{s}},S_\tau^{\mathrm{q}}):
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}$.
We consider a generative model $G_\phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{M}_K$
with latent space $\mathcal{Z}$, a fixed architecture, and trainable weights $\phi$.
Let $\widehat\phi$ denote its weights learned from $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$,
and write $M(z):=G_{\widehat\phi}(z)$ for the mask associated with a
latent code $z\in\mathcal{Z}$.
Within the masks represented by this learned generator, we seek one
that supports accurate task-specific predictors across the training
tasks. This gives the ideal latent solution and shared mask
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        z^\star &\in \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}
        \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tr}}|}
        \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tr}}}
        \mathcal{L}_{S_\tau^{\mathrm{q}}}
        \!\left(f_{\widehat\theta_\tau(M(z)),M(z)}\right), \\
        M^\star &:=G_{\widehat\phi}(z^\star).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:generative-transfer-objective}
\end{equation}
The value of this structure lies in its ability to transfer beyond
the tasks used to identify it. We therefore assess $M^\star$ through
the expected loss on a new task drawn independently from the same
environment:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{\tau,\,S_\tau^{\mathrm{s}},\,S_\tau^{\mathrm{q}}}
    \left[
        \mathcal{L}_{S_\tau^{\mathrm{q}}}
        \!\left(f_{\widehat\theta_\tau(M^\star),M^\star}\right)
    \right].
    \label{eq:transfer-query-loss}
\end{equation}
