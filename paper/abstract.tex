The connectivity and parameter-sharing structure of a neural network
influences how well it generalizes from limited data. We study how
solutions to related tasks can reveal structural constraints that
remain useful when numerical parameters are learned afresh. We
represent exact sharing by an indexator specifying permitted
connections and signed groups of shared weights, with task-specific
values supplied by a trainable codebook. For aligned ReLU weight matrices
with nonzero entries in the same positions, we characterize when the observations
uniquely determine the groups and give sufficient conditions for exact
recovery by Sign--Ratio Recovery (SRR). For fitted dense networks,
we propose Approximate Sign--Ratio Recovery (aSRR). It combines
directions from signed groups and source-task statistics into a
single weight basis. Each new task fits its own coefficients in
that basis. On a cross-correlation benchmark with known connections,
aSRR recovers all signed groups in six layouts. On six real datasets,
it attains the highest mean accuracy among the compared methods at
matched sharing budgets, averaging the two test views on the
augmented datasets. The statistical basis leads on transformed
Aug-Omniglot test images, and MSR-FC leads on clean Aug-miniImageNet
test images. Permuting the group assignments reduces accuracy on both
augmented datasets and UCI HAR.
