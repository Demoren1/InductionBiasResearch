The connectivity and parameter-sharing structure of a neural network influences how well it generalizes from limited data and how quickly its numerical parameters can be optimized.
We study how related tasks can reveal structural constraints that remain useful when numerical parameters are learned afresh.
We represent these constraints by an indexator specifying permitted connections and groups of shared weights, with task-specific values supplied by a trainable codebook.
Our central hypothesis is that structures extracted from source tasks can improve prediction on new tasks and help numerical optimization reach low-error solutions with less training.
We investigate when such constraints are identifiable, how they can be extracted jointly from task solutions, and how their transfer affects prediction and convergence under matched training budgets.
