Inductive biases shape how neural networks generalize from limited
data. Encoding these biases in network structure often requires domain
expertise. Our central hypothesis is that structural regularities shared
across solutions to related tasks provide an inductive bias for learning
new tasks. We represent such structure by permitted connections and
groups of weights tied up to sign. For aligned source weights with
known connections, we characterize when the tasks jointly determine
the groups despite neuron rescaling and give sufficient conditions for
exact recovery by Sign--Ratio Recovery (SRR). For fitted networks,
Approximate Sign--Ratio Recovery (aSRR) estimates the signed groups and
rotates their basis toward directions learned from source examples,
allowing the weights to deviate from exact ties. Each new task fits its
own coefficients in the transferred basis.
