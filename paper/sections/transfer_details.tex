\subsection{Transfer benchmark protocol}
\label{app:transfer-details}

For each cross-correlation layout we draw 96 source, 48 development
and 160 test functions. Each source function has 96 Gaussian inputs
and independent response noise with standard deviation 0.05. Source
least-squares fits use 80 examples, with the remaining 16 for
numerical validation. The output coordinates are observed, so aSRR
clusters the fitted profiles in their given order and scale. Each
target function has 96 noiseless query examples. For each support size,
development selects the representation and ridge strength after refit.
The paired intervals use the six layout means. Yeh and MSR-FC learn
their representations by differentiating through ridge refits on
source functions.

\paragraph*{Source directions for partial sharing.}
For each class in each source task, let $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$
be its means in the support and query samples, centered across
classes within each sample. Let $C_S$ and $C_Q$ be the analogous
class-centered sample covariance matrices. With
$\operatorname{sym}(A)=(A+A^{\mathsf T})/2$, the two signals are
\[
    M_1=\operatorname{sym}\mathbb E[\mathbf a\mathbf b^{\mathsf T}],
    \qquad
    M_2=\operatorname{sym}\mathbb E[C_S C_Q],
\]
where the averages run over classes and source tasks. We use $M_1$
or mix the two signals, each divided by its Frobenius norm, with
weight $1/4$ on $M_2$. For the resulting signal $M$, we take the
leading generalized eigenvectors of
\[
    M\mathbf u=\gamma
        \bigl(\Sigma+0.03\,\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)
            \mathrm{Id}/d\bigr)\mathbf u,
\]
where $\Sigma$ is the pooled within-class covariance and $d$ is the
input dimension at the site. We orthonormalize the retained
directions. For the input-group variants we also build guides that
keep $\max(2,\lfloor k/8\rfloor)$ signed directions from the
per-site Ward basis, ranked by their signal-to-variance ratio,
and solve the eigenproblem in their orthogonal complement. The
moment-based bank includes a guide formed by concatenating the
unconstrained and group-seeded bases, scaled by $\sqrt{3/4}$ and
$\sqrt{1/4}$, respectively. Equation~\eqref{eq:asrr-partial-basis}
uses these guides to complete Ward or Lloyd input groups.

For HAR, we estimate eight input directions from the source
fitting and structure-selection samples and lift them to the
global weight space with separate coefficients for each of the
16 neurons. We combine this guide with the signed global Ward
groups. On cross-correlation, the guide instead uses the leading
eigenvectors of the symmetrized cross moment of least-squares weight
estimates from two halves of each source task on the fixed support.

\paragraph*{Real-data episodes.}
Toroidal MNIST and Aug-Omniglot use 64 hidden neurons. On MNIST, we
split the 45 digit pairs into 25 source, 10 development and 10 test
pairs and separate the original images before any transformations.
We apply a random circular shift followed by the same fixed pixel
permutation for every image. The test set has 2,000 two-way episodes.
Aug-Omniglot has 48 source tasks, 192 development episodes and
768 test episodes with disjoint character pools. Its transformed
queries use random resized crops, reflections and rotations of up to
$30^\circ$.

FC100 uses 16 local image patches, with eight hidden neurons per
patch. Each patch is mean-pooled to $4\times4$ RGB values. We use 16
source tasks, 384 development episodes and 2048 test episodes.
On WISDM, we use 18 activities and nonoverlapping 64-sample windows
of the three smartwatch acceleration axes and their magnitude. The
32 hidden neurons are organized in four local time blocks. We use 27
source, 7 development and 12 test participants after filtering for
sufficient windows per activity. The 768 test episodes use early
windows for fitting and later windows for queries. For HAR we draw
360 episodes from the nine held-out participants and use the
development protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:asrr-tuning-details}.
These test episodes are drawn afresh from the same held-out pools
used in the earlier comparisons.

\paragraph*{Selection and comparisons.}
On the four input-group benchmarks, aSRR screens candidates on
about 48 development episodes at learning rates 0.003 and 0.01 for 75
and 150 Adam steps. Up to two candidates per family proceed to the
full development grid, with rates 0.001, 0.003, 0.01 and 0.03 and
durations of 25, 75, 150 and 300 steps. We use two numerical starts,
maximize mean development accuracy averaged equally over available
query views, and break ties by cross-entropy. The screened bank
also contains direct generalized-eigenvector completions with
uniform or reallocated site ranks. The reported partial fits
use the projected completions in~\eqref{eq:asrr-partial-basis}.
On Aug-Omniglot, the gradient bank includes all mixed allocations of
clean and transformed source queries across four blocks
(Appendix~\ref{app:asrr-gradient}).
Among the selected partial fits, toroidal MNIST uses Lloyd groups in
16 blocks and the mixed moments guide. FC100 uses the class-mean
guide with Ward groups shared across patches in two blocks, and
WISDM uses the same signal with Ward groups in eight blocks.

The baselines retain their previously selected models and numerical
settings. Their original four-panel screen forwarded up to three
candidates per method to the same full Adam grid and used transformed
queries for Aug-Omniglot selection. All methods are evaluated on
the same new episodes. Test accuracy averages two fresh starts on
the four input-group benchmarks. On HAR we use the selected numerical
ensemble for each method. Paired intervals use participant means on
HAR and WISDM, digit-pair means on toroidal MNIST and episodes
conditional on the held-out class pools on FC100 and Aug-Omniglot.

For each shuffled control, we permute the input assignments jointly
within each block or the global labels within each neuron and sign.
This preserves support, signs, and group sizes. In partial sharing,
we fix the source guide and rebuild the completed basis with the
permuted groups. We use the selected refit settings and the same
initialization seeds for aSRR and its four shuffles.

The four input-group panels use WSR and LUT-Q source proposals from eight
Dense fits selected by source numerical-validation loss. We retain
four Dense fits per source task on MNIST, Aug-Omniglot and WISDM, and
three on FC100. WSR uses the proximal map with rewinding.
LUT-Q updates a dictionary of nearest code values. Yeh learns
blockwise probabilistic assignments through three inner updates.
Its hard variant maximizes total assignment probability subject to
using the prescribed number of codes. MSR-FC learns a factorized
basis. The target fit uses the basis and learns fresh coefficients.
