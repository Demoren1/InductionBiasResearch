\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate recovery of known groups and transfer to new tasks.
We compare aSRR with Dense, WSR~\cite{shakerinava2024weight},
LUT-Q~\cite{cardinaux2018iteratively}, Yeh's method
\cite{yeh2022equivariance}, and MSR-FC~\cite{zhou2021meta}.
All sharing methods use the same first-layer coefficient budget
within each experiment. Yeh (hard) transfers discrete assignments.
Yeh (soft), MSR-FC and aSRR transfer fixed bases. Dense SVD and the
statistical basis transfer directions from Dense fits and source
statistics, respectively. Dense fits an independent weight for every
permitted connection. Each method fits fresh numerical parameters on the
same target examples.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery and transfer}

Following the cross-correlation experiments of
\citet{zhou2021meta,yeh2022equivariance}, we regress 68 outputs on
70 inputs. Each output uses three adjacent inputs. At each of the
three offsets, 51 positions share one coefficient and 17 positions
have their own, giving 54 independent coefficients. We draw six
independent layouts of the exceptions and give every method the same
204 permitted connections. The coefficients vary across functions.
We fit them by ridge regression from one or five training examples
on each new function.

Development retains the unrotated signed basis in all six layouts.
aSRR recovers every signed pair in all six layouts, and WSR recovers five
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer-linear}). With one training example, mean test
MSE is 0.1566 for aSRR, 0.1743 for MSR-FC and 0.1742 for
Meta-SP~\citep{zhang2026metasp}. The paired 95\% intervals for the
reduction with aSRR are $[0.0159,0.0194]$ against MSR-FC and
$[0.0160,0.0194]$ against Meta-SP across layouts.
With five training examples, Dense can estimate all three permitted
weights per output and reaches near-zero test MSE.

\subsection{Transfer on real data}

On toroidal MNIST, we split tasks by digit pair, using cyclic shifts
and a fixed pixel permutation following~\citet{shakerinava2024weight}.
On Aug-Omniglot and Aug-miniImageNet~\cite{zhou2021meta}, we fit on
clean training images. We evaluate clean and transformed views of
each validation image and each test image.
FC100~\cite{oreshkin2018tadam} splits tasks by image superclass.
UCI HAR~\cite{reyes2013har} and WISDM~\cite{weiss2019wisdm} split by
participant. Appendix~\ref{app:transfer-details} gives the splits and
refit protocol.

Development accepts the proposed rotation on all six datasets. On
Aug-Omniglot, four blocks use clean validation images and twelve use
transformed validation images to compute both source signals.
On the common test episodes in Table~\ref{tab:transfer-classification},
aSRR leads on six of eight conditions. The statistical basis leads
on transformed Aug-Omniglot images by 0.64 percentage points, and
MSR-FC leads on clean Aug-miniImageNet images by 0.15 points. With
equal weight on the clean and transformed results, aSRR has the
highest mean accuracy on both augmented datasets.
Tables~\ref{tab:bolt-diagonal} and~\ref{tab:aug-omniglot-anchored}
report comparisons with a common frozen Dense anchor.

Permuting the group assignments reduces accuracy on Aug-Omniglot by
3.31 points on clean images and 0.71 on transformed images, with paired
95\% intervals of $[3.02,3.59]$ and $[0.39,1.02]$. The reductions on
FC100, WISDM and transformed Aug-miniImageNet are 0.13, 0.32 and 0.18
points, with intervals of $[0.02,0.24]$, $[0.05,0.60]$ and $[0.07,0.29]$.
The other paired intervals include zero. This control measures the
contribution of assigning the learned signed groups to specific
inputs at the same coefficient budget.
