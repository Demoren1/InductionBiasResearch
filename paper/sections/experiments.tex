\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate recovery of known groups and transfer to new tasks.
We compare aSRR with Dense, WSR~\cite{shakerinava2024weight},
LUT-Q~\cite{cardinaux2018iteratively}, Yeh's method
\cite{yeh2022equivariance}, and MSR-FC~\cite{zhou2021meta}.
All sharing methods use the same first-layer coefficient budget
within each experiment. Yeh (hard) transfers discrete assignments.
Yeh (soft), MSR-FC and aSRR transfer fixed bases. Dense fits an
independent weight at every active position. Each method fits
fresh numerical parameters on the same target examples.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery and transfer}

Following the cross-correlation experiments of
\citet{zhou2021meta,yeh2022equivariance}, we regress 68 outputs on
70 inputs. Each output uses three adjacent inputs. At each of the
three offsets, 51 positions share one coefficient and 17 positions
have their own, giving 54 independent coefficients. We draw six
independent layouts of the exceptions and give every method the same
support of 204 connections. The coefficients vary across functions.
We fit them by ridge regression from one or five support examples
on each new function.

Development selects $\lambda=1$ and $\beta=0$ in all six layouts
at both support sizes. aSRR recovers every signed pair in all six
layouts, and WSR recovers five layouts
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer-linear}). With one example, mean query
MSE is 0.1566 for aSRR and 0.1743 for MSR-FC. The paired reduction
is 0.0177 with a 95\% interval of $[0.0159,0.0194]$ across layouts.
With five examples, Dense can estimate each of the three weights
per output on the known support and reaches near-zero query error.

\subsection{Transfer on real data}

We hold out digit pairs on toroidal MNIST, with cyclic shifts and a
fixed pixel permutation following~\citet{shakerinava2024weight}.
On Aug-Omniglot and Aug-miniImageNet~\cite{zhou2021meta}, we adapt
on clean images and evaluate the same query images in clean and
transformed form.
FC100~\cite{oreshkin2018tadam} holds out image superclasses. UCI
HAR~\cite{reyes2013har} and WISDM~\cite{weiss2019wisdm} hold out
participants. Appendix~\ref{app:transfer-details} gives the splits
and refit protocol.

The common development rule selects $\lambda=1$ on Aug-Omniglot
and $\lambda=1/8$ on the other five datasets. The query allocation
uses four clean and twelve transformed blocks on Aug-Omniglot.
On the common test episodes in Table~\ref{tab:transfer-classification},
aSRR improves over Yeh (soft) by 2.35 percentage points on toroidal
MNIST, 2.61 and 1.12 on the two Aug-Omniglot query sets, and 6.22 on
HAR. It improves over MSR-FC by 1.22 points on FC100 and by 0.53
on WISDM. The paired 95\% interval on WISDM is $[-0.18,1.23]$.
In three additional splits covering 34 other test participants,
both methods are retrained and aSRR gains 0.79 points
with interval $[0.50,1.09]$.
On Aug-miniImageNet, MSR-FC leads by 0.18 points on clean queries
($[0.01,0.36]$), and aSRR leads by 0.55 on transformed queries
($[0.37,0.73]$). Averaging both views gives aSRR a 0.18-point
advantage ($[0.05,0.32]$).

Rebuilding the basis after permuting the groups reduces accuracy
by 4.20 and 3.91 points on the clean and transformed Aug-Omniglot
queries and by 0.24 on HAR. The corresponding paired 95\% intervals
are $[3.90,4.50]$, $[3.58,4.23]$ and $[0.09,0.39]$. On
Aug-miniImageNet the reductions are 0.16 points on clean queries
($[0.04,0.27]$) and 0.19 on transformed queries ($[0.08,0.31]$).
These differences measure the contribution of the learned group
assignments at the same coefficient budget. The paired intervals
include zero on toroidal MNIST, FC100 and WISDM.
