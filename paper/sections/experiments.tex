\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate recovery of known groups and transfer to new tasks.
We compare aSRR with Dense, WSR~\cite{shakerinava2024weight},
LUT-Q~\cite{cardinaux2018iteratively}, Yeh's method
\cite{yeh2022equivariance}, and MSR-FC~\cite{zhou2021meta}.
All sharing methods use the same first-layer coefficient budget
within each experiment. Yeh (hard) transfers discrete assignments.
Yeh (soft), MSR-FC and aSRR transfer fixed bases. Dense SVD and the
statistical basis transfer directions from Dense fits and source
statistics, respectively. Dense fits an independent weight for every
permitted connection. Each method fits fresh numerical parameters on the
same target examples.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery and transfer}

Following the cross-correlation experiments of
\citet{zhou2021meta,yeh2022equivariance}, we regress 68 outputs on
70 inputs. Each output uses three adjacent inputs. At each of the
three offsets, 51 positions share one coefficient and 17 positions
have their own, giving 54 independent coefficients. We draw six
independent layouts of the exceptions and give every method the same
204 permitted connections. The coefficients vary across functions.
We fit them by ridge regression from one or five training examples
on each new function.

Development selects $\lambda=1$ and $\beta=0$ in all six layouts
at both training sample sizes. aSRR recovers every signed pair in all six
layouts, and WSR recovers five layouts
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer-linear}). With one training example, mean test
MSE is 0.1566 for aSRR, 0.1743 for MSR-FC and 0.1742 for
Meta-SP~\citep{zhang2026metasp}. The paired 95\% intervals for the
reduction with aSRR are $[0.0159,0.0194]$ against MSR-FC and
$[0.0160,0.0194]$ against Meta-SP across layouts.
With five training examples, Dense can estimate all three permitted
weights per output and reaches near-zero test MSE.

\subsection{Transfer on real data}

On toroidal MNIST, we split tasks by digit pair, using cyclic shifts
and a fixed pixel permutation following~\citet{shakerinava2024weight}.
On Aug-Omniglot and Aug-miniImageNet~\cite{zhou2021meta}, we fit on
clean training images. We evaluate clean and transformed views of
each validation image and each test image.
FC100~\cite{oreshkin2018tadam} splits tasks by image superclass.
UCI HAR~\cite{reyes2013har} and WISDM~\cite{weiss2019wisdm} split by
participant. Appendix~\ref{app:transfer-details} gives the splits and
refit protocol.

The common development rule selects $\lambda=1$ on Aug-Omniglot
and $\lambda=1/8$ on the other five datasets. On Aug-Omniglot,
four blocks use clean validation images and twelve use transformed
validation images.
On the common test episodes in Table~\ref{tab:transfer-classification},
aSRR improves over the statistical basis by 1.25 percentage points on
toroidal MNIST, 0.73 on FC100 and 0.33 on HAR. On WISDM, aSRR improves
over MSR-FC by 0.53 points with a paired 95\% interval of $[-0.18,1.23]$.
In three additional splits covering 34 other test participants,
both methods are retrained and aSRR gains 0.79 points
with interval $[0.50,1.09]$.

On Aug-Omniglot, aSRR leads Dense SVD by 0.32 points on clean test images
($[0.07,0.58]$). The statistical basis leads aSRR by 2.54 points on
transformed test images ($[2.05,3.03]$), while aSRR leads by 14.72 points
on clean test images.
With a common frozen Dense anchor, aTLAS gains 1.55 percentage
points over aSRR on transformed Aug-Omniglot test images, with a
difference of $-0.05$ points on clean test images
(Table~\ref{tab:aug-omniglot-anchored}).
On Aug-miniImageNet, MSR-FC leads by 0.18 points on clean test images
($[0.01,0.36]$), and aSRR leads by 0.55 on transformed test images
($[0.37,0.73]$). Averaging both views gives aSRR a 0.18-point
advantage ($[0.05,0.32]$).
At the same budget, aSRR improves over the statistical basis $\mathbf U$
by 0.75 points on clean test images and 0.71 on transformed test
images. The gains over Dense SVD are 1.61 and 2.36 points, respectively.
Table~\ref{tab:bolt-diagonal} gives the results of diagonal BOLT with
128 coefficients and a fixed Dense anchor.

Rebuilding the basis after permuting the groups reduces accuracy
by 4.20 and 3.91 points on the clean and transformed Aug-Omniglot
test images and by 0.24 on HAR. The corresponding paired 95\% intervals
are $[3.90,4.50]$, $[3.58,4.23]$ and $[0.09,0.39]$. On
Aug-miniImageNet the reductions are 0.16 points on clean test images
($[0.04,0.27]$) and 0.19 on transformed test images ($[0.08,0.31]$).
These differences measure the contribution of the learned group
assignments at the same coefficient budget. The paired intervals
include zero on toroidal MNIST, FC100 and WISDM.
