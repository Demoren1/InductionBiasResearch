\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate recovery of known groups and transfer to new tasks.
We compare aSRR and its hard Ward variant with Dense,
WSR~\cite{shakerinava2024weight}, LUT-Q~\cite{cardinaux2018iteratively},
Yeh's method~\cite{yeh2022equivariance}, and MSR-FC~\cite{zhou2021meta}.
Within each experiment, all sharing methods have the same first-layer
coefficient budget. A partial basis is stored and held fixed during
target fitting. Yeh (hard) transfers discrete assignments, while
Yeh (soft) and MSR-FC transfer continuous bases. Dense fits independent
weights on the same network support. Each method fits fresh numerical
parameters on the same target examples.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery and transfer}

Following the cross-correlation experiments of
\citet{zhou2021meta,yeh2022equivariance}, we regress 68 outputs on
70 inputs. Each output uses three adjacent inputs. At each of the
three offsets, 51 positions share one coefficient and 17 positions
have their own, giving 54 independent coefficients. We draw six
independent layouts of the exceptions and give every method the same
support of 204 connections. Within each layout, the coefficients vary
across functions. We fit them by ridge regression from one or five
support examples on each new function.

Development selects hard Ward sharing in all six layouts and at
both support sizes. It recovers all six indexators, and WSR recovers
five (Table~\ref{tab:transfer-linear}). With one example,
aSRR matches the true groups' MSE. The mean MSE reduction over WSR
is $0.015$, with a paired 95\% interval of $[-0.023,0.052]$
across layouts. With five examples, Dense can estimate each of the
three weights per output on the common support and reaches near-zero
error on the noiseless targets.

\subsection{Transfer on real data}

We hold out digit pairs on toroidal MNIST, with cyclic shifts and a
fixed pixel permutation following~\citet{shakerinava2024weight}.
On Aug-Omniglot~\cite{zhou2021meta}, we adapt on clean images and
evaluate the same query drawings in clean and transformed form.
FC100~\cite{oreshkin2018tadam} holds out image superclasses. UCI
HAR~\cite{reyes2013har} and WISDM~\cite{weiss2019wisdm} hold out
participants. Appendix~\ref{app:transfer-details} describes the task
splits and development selection.

Development selects hard sharing on Aug-Omniglot and partial sharing
on the other four datasets. The same Aug-Omniglot model uses one
block of groups built from clean-query gradients and three built
from transformed-query gradients. On the common test episodes in
Table~\ref{tab:transfer-classification},
aSRR gains 3.50 percentage points over Yeh (soft) on toroidal MNIST,
1.48 and 2.40 on the two Aug-Omniglot query sets, and 6.57 on HAR.
It gains 1.10 and 0.77 points over MSR-FC on FC100 and WISDM.
All six paired 95\% intervals are above zero.

Hard Ward sharing leads by 0.98 points on clean Aug-Omniglot
queries. The selected groups gain 5.70 points on transformed
queries. We shuffle the assignments while preserving support, signs,
group sizes and refit settings. For partial sharing we complete a new
basis with the same source guide. Averaging four shuffles reduces
accuracy by 5.60 and 6.59 points on the two Aug-Omniglot query sets
and by 0.16 on FC100 (paired 95\% interval $[0.05,0.27]$). The
intervals include zero on HAR and WISDM. On toroidal MNIST the shuffle
gains 0.36 points.
