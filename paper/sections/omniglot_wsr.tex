\subsection{Transfer on unaugmented Omniglot}
\label{sec:omniglot-wsr}

We use five-way Omniglot~\cite{lake2015human} tasks with 10 fitting,
five numerical-selection and five query images per class. Characters
are disjoint across source, development and query splits. We keep the
previously selected aSRR and WSR assignments and compare all methods
on the same 1024 test episodes. Numerical fitting uses 24 Adam starts
of 500 steps and averages the predictions of the four solutions
selected by within-task validation.

For WSR we apply the proximal update separately to each of the same 16
source Dense fits and set its strength to obtain 1024 groups of equal
weights. Development selects one grouping. We transfer its assignments
and learn fresh code values on every new task.
\input{sections/omniglot_comparison_results.tex}

Yeh (soft) achieves the highest accuracy of $83.79\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:omniglot-wsr}), with a paired gain of $0.71\pm0.51$
percentage points over aSRR. WSR and aSRR attain similar accuracy at
1024 codes. On 4096 additional episodes with both assignments fixed,
the paired WSR minus aSRR difference is $0.11$ percentage points
(95\% CI $[-0.11, 0.32]$).

For source construction, aSRR builds one grouping from all 16 saved
Dense fits on CPU, while WSR fits one candidate per source using a V100
and CPU proximal steps. The times are 0.41 and 23.76 seconds, a factor
of about 58 for this construction stage before development selection.
In a separate timing comparison on 128 shared target episodes, numerical
fitting and prediction take 23.51 seconds for aSRR and 22.84 for WSR on
the same V100. These target times average two runs per method in
reversed order.
