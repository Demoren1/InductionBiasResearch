\paragraph{Recovery and prediction are different criteria.}
Our method recovers the generating Pattern indexator in all six cases
and the clean DeepSets indexator in all three cases
(Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-recovery}). LUT-Q matches the Pattern result
and recovers two of the three clean DeepSets indexators. Nevertheless,
LUT-Q has lower clean DeepSets query MSE, $1.29\times10^{-4}$ versus
$6.10\times10^{-4}$. Finite numerical fitting can leave residual error
even when the selected structure admits the generating function;
recovery alone does not guarantee that optimization reaches it.
Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark-transfer} separates sample size from numerical
training progress.

With source noise $0.1$, neither method exactly recovers any of the
three DeepSets indexators. Our frozen banks contain no generating
indexator in these cases, so functional selection cannot recover it
from those banks. Two selected masks remain exact; the third contains
117 active connections instead of 32. Thus the remaining failure
includes candidate coverage and sharing, rather than only codebook
optimization. Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark-deepsets} displays the first
registered family under both noise conditions. Binary Pattern
follow-up results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:binary}.

\paragraph{Real-data transfer.}
Our Omniglot accuracy is $37.73\%$ with 64 independent first-layer
values, compared with Dense at $37.14\%$ with 12544 first-layer weights.
The selected mask is fully connected; the transferred restriction here
is sharing rather than sparsity.
The paired difference is $+0.59$ percentage points, with a 95\% interval
of approximately $[-0.02,1.19]$, conditional on one source cohort.
This supports competitive transfer with a smaller numerical family,
but does not establish an accuracy advantage over Dense. All absolute
accuracies concern the narrow fully connected setting here. The
published MSR result of $97.7\pm0.2\%$ on augmented 5-way 5-shot
Omniglot~\cite{zhou2021meta} uses a CNN, learned numerical initialization
and a much larger meta-training budget; it is contextual evidence,
not a matched row in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-real}.

On paired MNIST sums, our 128-code structure has MSE 1.185 versus
Dense at 1.058, a paired increase of 0.127 with a 95\% interval of
approximately $[0.087,0.167]$. The other tested 128-dimensional
structural families also trail Dense. This experiment does not
demonstrate useful sharing at the tested code budgets.

\paragraph{Conditional diagnosis on MNIST.}
Before opening target responses, development loss revealed an 11.1\%
gap to Dense. We therefore evaluated the source-only hard-assignment
refinement in Appendix~\ref{app:functional}. Its own source
numerical-selection MSE fell from 0.574 to 0.433, but development
transfer worsened from 1.203 to 1.238. It was not adopted. Its frozen
test MSE, 1.192, also fails to improve the original 1.185.

Keeping the learned mask and releasing equality constraints gives
test MSE 1.062, close to Dense at 1.058. A random mask preserving every
hidden neuron's degree gives 1.145. This isolates useful connectivity
from the limitations of the selected sharing constraints at 128 codes.
The mask control has 627 independent first-layer values, versus 128
for sharing and 3136 for Dense, so it is a larger-budget diagnostic.
It does not verify a true neural indexator, which is unavailable on
real data. Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark-real} includes all conditional
controls rather than replacing the primary row with a favorable one.

\paragraph{Computation.}
Full measurements are in Appendix~\ref{app:costs}. On the representative
DeepSets family, fresh adaptation plus prediction takes 0.993 seconds
per task for our structure versus 1.212 for Dense Adam plus Gauss--Newton;
Dense Adam alone takes 0.775 seconds and has higher error. On Omniglot,
our packed target cost is 2.75 ms per task versus Dense at 1.58 ms.
On MNIST it is 29.7 versus 30.9 ms, but peak target allocation is
1096 versus 71 MiB: the current basis implementation materializes
large intermediate feature tensors. Fewer independent parameters
therefore do not imply universally faster or smaller-memory execution.
Extraction is an additional offline cost, and the incomplete operator
counts must not be interpreted as total hardware FLOPs.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Three synthetic families and one source cohort per real benchmark do
not quantify variability over arbitrary task environments. The real
protocols, signed dictionaries and common fully connected architectures
are disclosed adaptations of the original work. Our results support
exact extraction and transfer in the clean synthetic setting and
competitive few-shot transfer on the reduced Omniglot setting, while
exposing failures under noise and restrictive real-data sharing.
